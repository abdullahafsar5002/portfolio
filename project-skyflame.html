Case study 01

SkyFlame
ERP & Dealer Portal

Asia Gas Appliances, Karachi · 2025–present · In production

A gas-appliances business was running its stock, its dealers and its payroll on spreadsheets and goodwill. SkyFlame replaced that with a system where the numbers are correct by construction — not correct because nobody noticed a mistake yet.

The problem

Stock, purchases, production runs, dealer orders and payroll were separate concerns held together by one person remembering what happened last week. The business moved physical goods through a production line, and every one of those documents moved quantities. Any drift between the documents and the stored number was invisible until a customer complained.

How it fits together

Scope

Twenty-plus modules covering sales and invoicing, purchases, inventory, bill of materials and production, dealer orders, returns, payments, cheques, worker payroll and reporting. It ships as a desktop application — Electron over Node.js, SQLite on disk — with a companion dealer portal served from Cloudflare Workers and backed by D1.

01 — Stock correctness lives in the database

The obvious design is a stock column you can edit. It fails quietly: one UPDATE, one forgotten import, and the number no longer matches the documents behind it — with nothing in the system recording when it stopped being true.

Instead, quantities are never edited. Every movement is an append-only ledger row, and triggers reject a silent edit outright — if a figure is wrong, a compensating entry has to exist to correct it. Foreign keys and multi-table transactions mean a quantity cannot drift from the invoice, purchase order or production run that moved it. The current stock is a derived fact, not a stored opinion.

02 — The server holds authority, not the client

The dealer portal is the attack surface that mattered. Anything a dealer can send — a price, an order line, a return amount — is treated as an input, never a fact. Pricing and ownership resolve on the server for every request, so a dealer cannot set their own rate, reorder another dealer's invoice, or inflate a refund. Submissions arrive as pending and are approved in the office.

03 — 472 tests, aimed at the expensive failures

The suite covers pricing, stock movement, portal authentication, and the full order and return lifecycle. It is deliberately concentrated on the money and inventory paths. A checkout that 404s gets fixed in a day; a refund that pays twice does not announce itself.

04 — Recoverable by construction

Encrypted automatic backups with restore validation, plus safe points before risky operations, so a mistaken change is always recoverable. Around that: scrypt hashing, rotating refresh tokens, per-device sync credentials with individual revocation, and a strict Content-Security-Policy.

Result

Twenty-plus modules in daily production use, one database that keeps its own invariants, and 472 tests protecting the paths where a mistake costs money.
