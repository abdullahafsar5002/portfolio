Toruk Makto
League

torukmaktoleague.com · Live production site

A league site is a database problem wearing a website's clothes. The content is fixed and public; the hard part is letting a visitor find a thing without guessing the URL.

The problem

A golf league accumulates content that keeps changing: players join, rosters shift, fixtures land, venues get booked. The naive build lists all of it flat and leaves the visitor to work out what matters. A standings table that is one screen away from nothing is worse than no standings table.

What it ships

The public site surfaces teams, standings, venues, partners, a photo gallery, an FAQ and registration flows. The site also credits the build explicitly, which matters for a site that is itself a portfolio piece.

01 — Grouping is the whole design

Rosters are grouped so a team reads as a team, fixtures are grouped so a date reads as a date, and venues are presented as places rather than addresses in a list. None of that is exotic, and all of it is the difference between a site people can use and a site they scroll past.

02 — Responsive, not shrunken

Layouts were built to behave intentionally across phone, tablet and desktop. A fixtures table that merely scales down is unreadable on the device most visitors actually use.

03 — Production means maintained

The site is deployed and kept live. On a content site the real failure modes are broken links, stale seasons and content that stopped being true months ago — none of which show up in a demo.
