Most visitors open a restaurant menu on a phone, one-handed, outdoors, while hungry. That constraint decided everything else.

The problem

A menu site has one job: let someone decide. Everything that competes with that decision — carousels, autoplay, pop-ups, an eight-step checkout for six items — works against it. The ordering side has the opposite problem. Building card payment infrastructure for a restaurant that already takes orders on WhatsApp is engineering effort spent on the wrong thing.

01 — Mobile-first, not mobile-friendly

Layouts were designed for a small screen first and scaled up, rather than desktop-first with a breakpoint bolted on. The menu stays legible at narrow widths and items remain tappable without precise aiming.

02 — Clarity as a feature

Visible pricing, clear descriptions and a menu structure that a first-time visitor can follow without instructions. Nothing on the page competes with the food.

03 — Order where the customer already is

Ordering ends in WhatsApp rather than a bespoke checkout. The customer stays on the platform they already use, the restaurant gets the order in the form it already handles, and neither side has to trust a payment integration that was not the problem.

04 — Deployed and maintained

Built on HTML, CSS, Bootstrap and JavaScript, then deployed and maintained through GitHub Pages — which keeps hosting cost at zero and the update path to a single push.
