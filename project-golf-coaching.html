Afsar Ali
Golf Coaching

coach.abdullahafsar.site · Live service website

A coach sells a lesson before the visitor knows the coach. The site has one job: make a stranger trust the method enough to message you.

The problem

Private coaching is a trust purchase made by someone who cannot evaluate the product before buying it. There is no trial, no cart and no returns policy. The visitor is deciding between "book this person" and "keep looking", and the evidence for the first option has to exist somewhere on the page.

What it ships

Eight pages: home, about, programs, pricing, gallery, testimonials, FAQ and contact. Each one removes a different objection — programs answer "what do I actually get", pricing removes the fear of asking, testimonials answer "has this worked for someone like me", and FAQ absorbs the questions that would otherwise stall a decision.

01 — Programs, not services

Beginner lessons, junior coaching, private coaching and swing analysis are presented as distinct products with their own outcomes. "Coaching" is abstract; "build confidence and fundamentals" is something a visitor can picture themselves getting.

02 — One clear priority per session

The site's own philosophy is "clarity first, confidence second" — one priority per session, video feedback, measurable progress. The content is structured around that argument rather than listing everything the coach can possibly do.

03 — Booking ends in WhatsApp

Every booking path routes to a pre-filled WhatsApp message. For a local service business the customer is already in WhatsApp, and a message thread keeps the conversation, the booking and the follow-up in one place. No third-party booking platform taking a cut, no account to create.

04 — Reachable from the page

WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook are linked from the site, with a newsletter sign-off for people not ready to book. A visitor who is not ready today is still reachable tomorrow.
